# Cover Letter Principles

> Reference for `cover_letter_writer`.

## When a cover letter actually matters
Most are skimmed or ignored — but they swing the decision in specific cases. Spend real effort when:
- The role is a **stretch** (the letter connects dots the resume can't — a `Stretch` band from `resume_match_scorer`).
- There's a **narrative pivot** to explain (domain switch, archetype that isn't an obvious fit).
- The application is via **referral or warm intro** (the letter is read).
- The company **explicitly asks** for one, or is mission-driven (frontier labs read "why us" closely).
- It's a small/early company where the hiring manager reads everything.

For a high-volume application through a Workday wall, a tight 3-paragraph letter is enough; don't over-invest.

## The job of the letter
The resume proves *what* he did. The letter argues *why it matters for THIS role* and *why this company* — the two things bullets can't say in his voice. It is an argument, not a summary. Never restate the resume.

## Structure (3–4 short paragraphs, < 350 words)

1. **The hook (why this role, specifically).** Open with the most relevant proof point tied to the role's *unstated #1 priority* (from the JD Profile). Not "I am writing to apply for..." — open with the thing that makes him obviously relevant.
2. **The proof (one story, not a list).** Pick ONE story from `story_bank.md` / `project_inventory.md` that maps to the role's core challenge, told as situation → what he did → outcome. One vivid, quantified story beats three thin ones.
3. **The fit (why this company / mission).** Specific to the company — reference their actual product, strategy, or mission (pull from `companies/{name}/` if a dossier exists via `company_researcher`). Generic "I admire your culture" is worse than omitting this.
4. **The close.** Brief, confident, forward-looking. One line.

## Archetype tuning (match the JD Profile archetype)
- **Frontier AI Lab** → lead with alignment/safety conviction + a point of view on the problem; mission fit is heavily weighted. Show first-principles thinking.
- **AI Platform** → lead with research-to-production and developer/enterprise impact.
- **Consumer / Marketplace** → lead with growth, experimentation, and a metric that moved.
- **Enterprise Tech** → lead with GTM, cross-functional scale, and regulated-domain credibility.

## Voice (Vikash)
Direct, builder-oriented, evidence-backed, no fluff. The same voice as his LinkedIn (`linkedin_optimizer`) and stories. Specifically:
- First person, conversational but senior. Short sentences.
- Concrete nouns and numbers over adjectives.
- No buzzwords (passionate, innovative, synergy, thought leader).
- No groveling and no arrogance — peer-to-peer confidence.

## Non-negotiables
- **Same facts as the resume and sources** — every claim traces to `career_sources.md`; no new invented metrics. The letter and resume must not contradict each other.
- **One story, told well** — resist listing accomplishments.
- **Genuinely company-specific paragraph 3** — if you can't say something specific and true about the company, the letter isn't ready; do the research first.
- **< 350 words.** A long cover letter is an unread cover letter.
