# Story: The Hiring Miss on PETs

## 10-second hook (BLUF)
On the PETs industry-standards effort, I failed twice early: I couldn't hire for a role I'd scoped wrong, and I let the first proposals go out too slowly — and both traced back to me not adjusting fast enough to how unusual this work was. I fixed the role definition and the operating pace, and the effort went on to produce the Mozilla-co-authored IPA standard.

## STAR+ (default delivery — S 10% · T 10% · A 60% · R 20%)

**Situation** — The PETs / IPA work (post-iOS14 privacy-preserving measurement) was unlike normal product PM work — it was part standards diplomacy, part deep cryptography, part industry education. Speed and the right people mattered enormously because we were trying to shape an industry window.

**Task** — I owned staffing the effort and getting our first proposals into the industry conversation quickly.

**Action** — Two genuine failures, owned:
- **The hiring miss.** I tried to hire against a fairly conventional PM profile, and candidates struggled — they couldn't articulate *impact* for work this ambiguous and externally-facing. The real problem was my role definition: this seat needed a different skill set (standards/industry-facing, comfortable with cryptographic ambiguity) than I'd specified. I had been screening for the wrong thing.
- **The slow proposals.** I didn't get our initial proposals out fast enough — I over-polished while an industry window was open, when rougher-but-earlier would have shaped the conversation more.
- What I changed: I **redefined the role** around the actual skill (and adjusted how I evaluated candidates for it), and I shifted to **shipping proposals earlier and iterating in the open** rather than perfecting first — the "bring options early" discipline applied to standards work.

**Result** — Once the role and the pace were right, the effort produced the **IPA proposal co-authored with Mozilla** and the founding of W3C PATCG. The lasting lesson: when work is genuinely novel, my *first* instincts about staffing and pace are the things to question hardest. `[NEEDS INPUT: how long the hiring gap / proposal delay actually ran — confirm before telling]`
