**Why this matters (from LinkedIn's JAMES title mapping model):**
LinkedIn's title normalization system (JAMES — "Job Title Mapping with Multi-Aspect Embeddings and Reasoning") maps every free-text title to a canonical **Taxonomy Entity ID** in their Economic Graph. When you select from the dropdown, you bypass the fuzzy NLP pipeline and lock in the exact entity. When you free-type a custom string (like adding parentheticals), LinkedIn's model has to guess your entity using syntactic + semantic + topological embeddings — and it may guess wrong, placing you in a lower seniority bucket.

The model uses three embedding layers to resolve your title:
1. **Syntactic** — string similarity ("Senior PM" ≈ "Senior Product Manager")
2. **Semantic** — BERT-based meaning ("Head of Product" ≈ "Director of Product")
3. **Topological** — Poincaré hyperbolic embeddings from 2.7M+ career transitions, capturing where this title sits in the seniority hierarchy

Picking from the dropdown guarantees layer 1 is a perfect match. Don't make the algorithm work harder than it needs to.

## Action 15: Stack Meta Roles (Career Trajectory Signal — JAMES Paper)

Go to: **LinkedIn → Profile → Experience**

Make sure all Meta roles appear **under one "Meta" company entry** with each role shown as a sub-position. This displays the promotion arc:
```
Meta (6 years)
  ├─ Senior Product Manager              (2024–2025)
  ├─ Lead Product Manager                (2023–2024)
  ├─ Head of Product                     (2021–2024)
  ├─ Product Lead                        (2019–2021)
  └─ Course Instructor                   (2022–2025)
```

**Why this is a hard quantitative signal, not just cosmetic (JAMES paper):**
LinkedIn's JAMES model builds a **directed Job Transition Graph** from 2.7M+ real career transitions. Each title is a node; each promotion/move is a directed edge. This graph is embedded into **Poincaré hyperbolic space**, where parent nodes (more recent/senior titles) sit closer to the center and child nodes (earlier/junior titles) sit at the periphery.

When your roles are stacked under one company, the algorithm traces your specific path through this graph:
```
Product Lead → Head of Product → Lead PM → Senior PM
```
Because millions of real users follow similar trajectories at Meta, the model assigns high confidence that your current title represents a **promoted** senior position, not a lateral or entry-level one. This mathematically increases your inferred seniority score in the Two-Tower DNN.

If roles are listed as **separate company entries**, the transition graph can't trace the path — each role looks like an independent job, not a promotion. The Poincaré embedding loses the hierarchical signal entirely.

If roles are currently listed as separate company entries, edit each one and ensure "Company" is set to the same "Meta" page.
